About

Where psychology meets technology, policy and development

“Behavioural insight becomes powerful when it improves real lives and institutions.”

Dr. Oluwatosin Eniola Ilevbare is a behavioural scientist with wide interests across health, energy utilisation, entrepreneurial attitude and the adoption of innovation. She is a Senior Lecturer at the Department of Psychology, University of Ilesa, Osun State, Nigeria, where she engages in research on maternal and child health, health-seeking behaviour, life satisfaction, mental health promotion and illness prevention. She is also the Director of the Entrepreneurial Skills and Innovation Centre at the University of Ilesa, Nigeria.

With near two decades of experience in policy research, manpower development, and technology management services for government and private sectors, Dr. Ilevbare holds a first-class Bachelor's degree, M.Sc and Ph.D in Psychology from Obafemi Awolowo University, Nigeria. She has published 36 research articles in reputable journals and has presented her scholarly work at national and international conferences, winning research and travel grants in the course of her career.

She has served as Principal Investigator and team member on national and international projects funded by UNESCO, the MacArthur Foundation, the Federal Government of Nigeria, the World Bank and similar organisations, with outputs published in Technical Reports, Policy Briefs and journal articles.

At NACETEM, she contributed to the documentation and validation of Nigerian indigenous knowledge as a pathway to sustainable development. Through the NISER Actualising Behaviour Change series, her work applies behavioural science to governance and anti-corruption.

She is a member of the Nigerian Psychological Association (NPA), American Psychological Association (APA), GLOBELICS, Africalics, Healthcare Administrators Association of Nigeria (HAAN), Organization for Women in Science in the Developing World (OWSD) and the Social Science Research Council (SSRC). Her research interests are principally directed toward contributing to Goal 3 of the SDGs by understanding human behaviour, improving individual health outcomes and population well-being.
